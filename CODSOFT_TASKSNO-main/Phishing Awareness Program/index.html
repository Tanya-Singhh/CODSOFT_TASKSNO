Don't Get Phished.

An interactive training module that teaches you how to identify phishing attacks, recognize social engineering, and protect your digital identity.

01 — INTRODUCTION

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack in which criminals impersonate trusted people, organizations, or services to trick victims into revealing sensitive information or performing an unsafe action.

📧

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to make you click links, open attachments, or provide sensitive information.

🌐

Website Phishing

Fake websites that imitate legitimate services such as banking, social media, shopping, or email platforms.

📱

Smishing

Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or messaging applications.

📞

Vishing

Voice-based scams in which attackers pretend to be banks, technical support teams, or authorities.

🎯

Spear Phishing

Highly targeted attacks designed specifically for a particular person or organization.

👔

Whaling

Phishing attacks targeting senior executives or high-value individuals.

02 — EMAIL SECURITY

How to Spot a Suspicious Email

Phishing emails often imitate trusted companies but contain small clues that reveal the deception.

From: Security Team <security@micr0soft-support.example>
Subject: URGENT: Your account will be suspended!

Dear Customer,

We detected unusual activity on your account. You must verify your account immediately or your account will be permanently suspended.

Click here to verify your account.

Thank you,
Account Security Team

🚩

Red Flag #1: Strange Sender

The sender's domain does not match the official organization.

🚨

Red Flag #2: Urgency

Attackers often create panic by claiming that something will happen immediately.

🔗

Red Flag #3: Suspicious Links

Hover over links before clicking and carefully inspect the destination.

✍️

Red Flag #4: Language Errors

Poor grammar, unusual wording, or generic greetings can indicate a fraudulent message.

Remember: Never provide passwords, OTPs, banking information, or other sensitive information just because an email asks for it.
03 — FAKE LOGIN PAGES

Can You Recognize a Fake Login Page?

A phishing page may look almost identical to a legitimate login page. Always verify the website address before entering credentials.

⚠️ https://secure-login-account.example-login.com

Account Login

Sign in to continue

🔎

Check the Domain

Look carefully at the domain name. Attackers may use misspellings or unrelated domains.

🔒

HTTPS Is Not Enough

HTTPS encrypts communication but does not automatically mean the website itself is legitimate.

🧠

Think Before Login

If you did not expect the login request, navigate to the service manually instead of following the message link.

Best Practice: Use bookmarks or manually type the official website address when accessing important accounts.
04 — FRAUDULENT WEBSITES

How to Identify a Fake Website

Fraudulent websites are designed to appear trustworthy. Check several indicators before interacting with them.

🌍

Incorrect Domain

Look for unusual domains, additional words, misspellings, or suspicious domain endings.

🖼️

Poor Design

Fake websites may contain broken images, inconsistent fonts, poor grammar, or low-quality graphics.

💳

Unexpected Payment Request

Be suspicious if a website suddenly requests sensitive payment or identity information.

📞

Missing Contact Information

Legitimate organizations generally provide reasonable ways to verify their identity and contact them.

⚡

Too-Good-To-Be-True Offers

Extremely large discounts, prizes, or rewards can be used as bait.

🔐

Unexpected Login Requests

Be careful when a website asks for credentials without a clear reason.

05 — SOCIAL ENGINEERING

The Human Side of Cybercrime

Social engineering attacks manipulate human emotions and decision-making instead of relying only on technical exploits.

⏰

Urgency

"Act immediately or your account will be closed." Attackers want victims to act before thinking.

🏆

Reward

Fake prizes, discounts, refunds, and giveaways can encourage victims to click.

👮

Authority

Criminals may impersonate managers, banks, government officials, or IT departments.

😨

Fear

Messages may threaten penalties, account suspension, legal action, or financial loss.

🤝

Trust

Attackers may impersonate friends, colleagues, classmates, or familiar organizations.

🎭

Impersonation

Criminals create believable identities to convince victims that a request is legitimate.

Golden Rule: If a message makes you feel unusually rushed, frightened, excited, or pressured, stop and verify it independently.
06 — CASE STUDIES

Real-World Phishing Examples

01
MICROSOFT 365 / BUSINESS EMAIL ATTACKS

Fake Account Verification

Attackers have commonly used messages pretending to be Microsoft or an organization's IT department. Victims are directed to a fake sign-in page designed to capture credentials.

Lesson: Verify unexpected account-security messages through an independently opened service.
02
BANKING / FINANCIAL PHISHING

Fake Bank Alert

A message may claim that suspicious activity was detected and ask the recipient to verify their account. The link can lead to a fraudulent page.

Lesson: Contact your bank using an official phone number or official application instead of the message link.
03
SOCIAL MEDIA

Fake Account Recovery

Attackers may send messages claiming that a social media account has violated a policy and requires immediate verification.

Lesson: Open the social media application directly and check account notifications there.
04
DELIVERY SCAMS

Fake Package Notification

A message may claim that a parcel could not be delivered and request payment or personal information to reschedule delivery.

Lesson: Check deliveries through the courier's official website or application.

11 — PROTECTION

Your Anti-Phishing Checklist

Follow these simple habits whenever you receive an unexpected message, email, or website request.

✓

Stop and think

Do not act immediately just because a message creates urgency.

✓

Verify the sender

Check the complete email address and domain.

✓

Inspect links

Hover over links and examine their destination before clicking.

✓

Use MFA

Enable multi-factor authentication wherever possible.

✓

Use strong unique passwords

Never reuse important passwords across different services.

✓

Keep software updated

Install security updates for operating systems, browsers, and applications.

✓

Report suspicious messages

Use your organization's reporting process or the service's official reporting tools.

✓

Never share authentication codes

Do not disclose passwords, OTPs, recovery codes, or MFA approval information.
